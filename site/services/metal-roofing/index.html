Standing Seam and Metal Roofing

A standing seam metal roof lasts 40 to 70 years — roughly two to three times an asphalt roof — because the fasteners are concealed inside the seam where weather never reaches them.

Standing seam vs exposed fastener

Standing seam hides the fasteners inside the raised vertical seam. Exposed-fastener panels are cheaper, but every screw carries a rubber washer that degrades, which means hundreds of future leak points. For a house, install standing seam.

Why it costs more up front

Two to three times the installed cost of architectural shingles. Over a 50-year window it is usually cheaper, because an asphalt roof will be replaced twice in that span. If you intend to sell within five years, the math does not favor it.

Noise and lightning

Both concerns are outdated. Over solid decking and underlayment, a metal roof is no louder than shingles in rain. Metal does not attract lightning, and because it is non-combustible it is the safer material if lightning does strike nearby.

Where it genuinely wins

Low-slope sections where shingles should not be used, historic standing-seam replacements, snow-shedding porch roofs, and modern architecture where the panel line is the design.

Metal Roofing — questions we get

How long does a metal roof last?

40 to 70 years for standing seam with a quality coating. Exposed-fastener panels last less because the rubber washers on the screws degrade.

Is a metal roof noisy when it rains?

No. Installed over solid decking with underlayment — which is how residential metal is installed — it is no louder than an asphalt shingle roof. The noise reputation comes from barns with bare metal over open purlins.

Does a metal roof attract lightning?

No. Metal roofing does not increase the chance of a lightning strike, and because it is non-combustible it reduces fire risk if one occurs nearby.
