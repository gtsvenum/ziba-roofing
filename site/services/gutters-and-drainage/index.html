Gutters, Downspouts, and Roof Drainage

Undersized or clogged gutters cause more foundation and fascia damage in the DMV than roof leaks do, because the water ends up against the house instead of away from it.

Sizing is not one-size

Five-inch K-style gutter is the default, but a steep roof with a large drainage area overwhelms it in a heavy summer storm. We size the gutter and the number of downspouts to the actual roof area and pitch. Overflowing gutters are frequently a sizing problem, not a clog.

Seamless aluminum

Formed on site to the exact length of your run, so there are no mid-run seams to split. The standard choice for most homes.

Half-round copper

For historic homes and anywhere the gutter is visible architecture. Copper lasts 50-plus years and weathers to a patina instead of peeling.

Where the water goes

A downspout dumping at the foundation is a wet basement waiting to happen. We extend discharge a minimum of four feet from the wall, or tie into buried drainage where the grade allows.

Gutters & Drainage — questions we get

How often should gutters be cleaned?

Twice a year in the DMV — late spring and late fall. Properties under oak or maple canopy need a third cleaning in midsummer.

Are gutter guards worth it?

Fine-mesh guards meaningfully reduce cleaning frequency under heavy tree cover. Foam inserts and plastic screens clog with shingle grit and pine needles and tend to make the problem worse.

Why do my gutters overflow even though they are clean?

They are probably undersized or short on downspouts for the roof area draining into them. Gutters should be sized to actual roof square footage and pitch, not installed at a default five inches everywhere.
