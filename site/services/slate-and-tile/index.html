Slate and Tile Roof Restoration

Natural slate roofs last 75 to 150 years, so a slate roof that is leaking usually needs targeted repair of the flashing and failed fasteners — not replacement.

Call (301) 366-8210 Free inspection

Most slate roofs fail at the nails, not the slate

The slate outlives the fasteners and the flashing by decades. When a 90-year-old slate roof starts leaking, the common causes are corroded nails letting individual slates slip, and valley or chimney flashing that has reached end of life. Replacing flashing and resetting slipped slates can buy another 30 years.

Repair done correctly

Individual slates are replaced with a slate hook or a copper bib — never face-nailed and never caulked. Face-nailing a replacement slate creates the next leak and marks the work as amateur from the ground.

Historic districts

Much of our service area sits in historic districts where the roofing material is regulated. We work to the applicable historic review standards and handle the documentation.

When replacement is honest

Widespread delamination — slates flaking in layers and sounding dull instead of ringing when tapped — means the slate itself is spent. At that point selective repair is throwing money at a finished roof, and we will tell you.

Slate & Tile Roofing — questions we get

How long does a slate roof last?

75 to 150 years depending on the quality of the stone. Vermont and Buckingham slate sit at the top of that range. The nails and flashing fail long before the slate.

Can a slate roof be repaired instead of replaced?

Usually yes. Slipped slates and failed valley or chimney flashing account for most slate roof leaks, and both are repairable. Replacement is only honest when the slate is delaminating across the roof.

Can you match my existing slate?

In most cases. Slate is matched by quarry, color, thickness, and size. Salvaged slate is often the best match on a historic roof.
