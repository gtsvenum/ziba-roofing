Flat and Low-Slope Roofing

Flat roofs are standard on DC rowhouses, and the three systems worth installing are TPO, EPDM rubber, and modified bitumen — each lasting roughly 20 to 30 years when the drainage and details are done correctly.

TPO

A heat-welded white thermoplastic membrane. The welded seams are the strongest detail in flat roofing, and the white surface reflects heat, which matters on a DC rowhouse with living space directly below the roof. Our default recommendation for most residential flat roofs.

EPDM rubber

Black synthetic rubber in large sheets with very few seams. Extremely durable and forgiving of movement, but it absorbs heat rather than reflecting it. A strong choice for roofs with no conditioned space directly beneath.

Modified bitumen

Torch-applied or self-adhered asphalt sheets in two or three plies. The most puncture-resistant option, which makes it the right pick for roofs that get walked on for HVAC service.

Drainage is the whole game

A flat roof is not flat — it needs a quarter inch per foot of fall to a drain or scupper. Standing water 48 hours after rain means the slope or the drain is wrong, and no membrane will survive that long term. We correct slope with tapered insulation rather than patching around ponding.

Flat & Low-Slope Roofing — questions we get

How long does a flat roof last?

A properly installed TPO, EPDM, or modified bitumen roof lasts 20 to 30 years. Failures before that are almost always drainage or flashing details, not the membrane itself.

What is the best flat roof material for a DC rowhouse?

TPO for most of them. The heat-welded seams outperform glued laps and the reflective white surface reduces cooling load on the top floor, which is usually finished living space in a rowhouse.

Why is water standing on my flat roof?

Insufficient slope or a blocked drain. A low-slope roof needs about a quarter inch per foot of fall. Water still sitting 48 hours after rain will shorten the life of any membrane and needs tapered insulation to correct.
